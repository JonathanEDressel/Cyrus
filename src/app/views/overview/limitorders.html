How quote-currency liquidity affects these orders

A limit order only fills if someone trades against it at your price, and how likely that is depends on the pair, not just the coin. The same coin can have a deep order book against one quote currency and almost nothing against another. Splitting a ladder across several quote currencies spreads that risk, but it also means some legs sit in thinner books, where they may rest far longer or fill only partially.

During a fast move, price can jump through several of your levels in one or two prints. In a deep book those levels fill on the way past. In a thin one the market can trade above your sell — or below your buy — without ever matching it, because there was not enough size at your exact price. The same mechanic cuts the other way: an outlying level can fill in full during a brief spike and then look badly priced once things settle.

Deepest USD, USDT — the reference books on nearly every venue. Tightest spreads and the best chance of a full fill.
Deep USDC — close to USDT depth on US venues such as Coinbase and Kraken; noticeably thinner on offshore venues where USDT dominates.
Moderate BTC, ETH — deep for large-cap alts, but the quote asset moves too, so your effective USD level drifts with BTC or ETH.
Thinner FDUSD, PYUSD, TUSD, DAI — each is deep on the venue that promotes it (FDUSD on Binance, PYUSD where PayPal pairs are listed) and sparse everywhere else.
Sparse BUSD, USDD — BUSD has been wound down and delisted on most venues; USDD trades thinly. Expect wide spreads and partial fills. Not good ladder rails.

Depth also varies by exchange, not just by quote currency. Cyrus lists the pairs your exchange actually offers and the minimums it enforces; it does not measure how much size is sitting in the book right now.
